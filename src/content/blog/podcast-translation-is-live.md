When Audioask launched in June, translation was on the list of things we were still working on. It is not on that list any more — it is **available to everyone, today**.

In practice, that means a podcast recorded in English, German or Japanese becomes readable — and listenable — in your own language. Here is what ships, and how to use it.

## What you get today

Three things, chained together so you do not have to think about them:

- **A translated transcript**, aligned with the original one, timestamp by timestamp. You keep both versions side by side.
- **A dubbed audio episode**, generated from that translation: a real audio file that keeps the pace of the conversation.
- **One voice per speaker**, picked from our library — or cloned from your own.

And because the translated transcript is indexed like any other, everything that already worked keeps working on it: full-text search, bookmarks, and [questions put to the episode](/en/features/ask) — in your language, with citations that point back to the right passage.

## Twelve languages

French, English, German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Arabic. All twelve are available for the translated text *and* for dubbed audio — there is no second-tier language.

## An audio episode, not just text

This is the part we are proudest of. Plenty of tools translate a transcript. Producing an episode you can actually listen to on your commute is a different problem.

Audioask detects the speakers, assigns each one a distinct voice, and generates a complete audio file. For a one-hour episode, expect a few minutes. What comes out sounds like an episode — not like a screen reader working through a document.

Pro users can go further and **clone their own voice**: a few minutes of recording are enough to create a clone you can reuse for every dub.

## Every translation benefits everyone

Like transcripts, translations feed the shared library. If someone has already translated an episode into your language, you get it instantly, without spending anything from your quota.

Put differently: the more the community translates, the less everyone waits. It is the same collaborative model that makes transcription fast on popular podcasts, applied to translation.

## Translate every new episode automatically

Translation combines with [automations](/en/features/automate). Set it up once, on a podcast you follow: every new episode gets transcribed, translated, dubbed, and dropped into your library — in the language you picked.

You wake up with today's episode already waiting in English. You did not start anything.

## Which plan do you need?

**Transcript translation is included in the Premium plan**, at €3/month. **Dubbed audio and voice cloning belong to the Pro plan.** Full quotas are on the [pricing page](/en/pricing), with the monthly / annual toggle.
